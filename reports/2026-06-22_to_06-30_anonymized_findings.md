# Anonymized Findings: Valko / Ao Yin Controversy, 2026-06-22 to 2026-06-30

## Purpose

This is a privacy-preserving public summary of a larger source-reconstruction project examining the Valko / Ao Yin implementation controversy in *Love and Deepspace*.

The underlying working material contains account-level verification records. Those records are kept outside the public layer. This file intentionally removes usernames, user IDs, post IDs, direct links to individual fan posts, and verbatim quotations that could make individual users easy to identify.

## Evidence scope

The working dataset contained **217 records** collected for the 2026-06-22 to 2026-06-30 period and relevant lead-up material.

During the final audit of the source set:

- **202 URLs** were re-checked directly through archived or preserved Sina pages.
- **15 remaining records** required separate review using official Weibo material, preserved related-post views, profile-history evidence, or other source traces.
- Records supported only by screenshots, related-post panels, deleted originals, or indirect traces were treated as lower-confidence evidence.

These counts describe the reconstructed source set. They **must not be interpreted as population estimates** of what most players or most members of any character fandom believed.

## Main findings

### 1. Opposition was not a single unified position

The recovered material does not support a simple story in which the five existing character fandoms acted as one bloc for one reason.

Before and immediately after the sixth-character announcement, different groups were already focused on different unresolved concerns, including:

- character-specific story and setting consistency
- allocation of production resources
- card and main-story scheduling
- mixed-banner dilution and spending strategy
- perceived reuse of character motifs or promotional resources
- localization and cultural-framing disputes
- broader distrust of company decision-making

Opposition to the new character sometimes overlapped with these concerns, but the underlying interests were not identical.

### 2. Existing-character fandom membership did not imply opposition to Valko

The source set also contains counterexamples: players who primarily supported an existing character but welcomed Valko / Ao Yin or expressed interest in the new character.

This matters because character-fandom identity and position on the implementation controversy were not mutually exclusive categories.

### 3. From June 23 to June 28, the dispute broadened substantially

Across the recovered material, discussion expanded from initial reactions to the new character into a wider set of issues:

- production capacity and allocation
- treatment of existing characters
- monetization and boycott strategy
- character autonomy and out-of-character concerns
- women's consumer agency and safety framing
- localization / domestic-cultural framing
- long-term trust in the operator

Boycott tactics were also heterogeneous. The reconstructed posts include several distinct approaches rather than one coordinated economic strategy.

### 4. The anti-company / withdrawal-oriented space pre-dated the Valko controversy

Material associated with a broader anti-company or withdrawal-oriented discourse was found from before the June Valko announcement.

This weakens the claim that this space was created by the Valko controversy itself.

At the same time, three categories must be kept separate:

- direct participation in a named community or tag space
- casual use of an anti-company label as an insult or shorthand
- broader overlap in themes such as production capacity, character consistency, women's agency, or company distrust

Shared language alone is not evidence of organizational membership or coordinated decision-making.

### 5. The A-0731 controversy appears to have formed through distributed roles

The June 29 escalation around the fictional "A-0731" label was not reconstructed as the action of a single identifiable originator.

The source sequence instead shows distinct functions emerging across different posts and accounts, including:

- early noticing / problem identification
- reframing the issue around historical or national trauma
- tagging or appealing to external media / public institutions
- connecting the issue to women's safety or corporate values
- adding further speculative associations
- high-volume amplification
- internal criticism warning against using historical trauma for fandom conflict

The available evidence therefore supports a **distributed propagation model** more strongly than a single-mastermind model.

### 6. External-mobilization pathways existed before A-0731

The reconstructed timeline contains attempts to appeal to media, public institutions, women's-safety discourse, and regulatory responsibility before the A-0731 frame became dominant.

A-0731 appears to have accelerated and consolidated existing external-mobilization pathways rather than creating them from zero.

### 7. Framing and amplification were separable processes

Several reconstructed chains show that the person who first raised a claim, the person who relayed it, and the person who converted it into a broader external-facing appeal were not necessarily the same.

For analysis, these should be treated as separate roles:

**discovery -> interpretation -> relay -> amplification -> external mobilization**

Collapsing these roles into a single "origin" can produce misleading attribution.

### 8. The coalition fragmented rapidly after cancellation

After the official cancellation announcement on June 30, the previously overlapping opposition space quickly split into different directions, including:

- return to character-specific demands
- continued refusal to spend or return
- criticism of the cancellation itself
- calls for Valko / Ao Yin to be restored
- claims of partial or strategic success

This rapid divergence is inconsistent with the idea of a stable coalition with one continuing objective.

## Analytical interpretation

The strongest interpretation supported by this reconstruction is:

> The controversy is better modeled as the temporary convergence of multiple pre-existing grievances, followed by rapid frame expansion and networked amplification, than as a single coordinated campaign with one shared motive.

This is an analytical conclusion from the recovered source set, not a claim about every player or every fandom member.

## Privacy transformation used for this public version

The public report removes or generalizes:

- fan usernames and handles
- user IDs
- individual post IDs
- direct links to fan posts
- verbatim fan quotations
- exact attribution of controversial claims to individual non-public persons
- unnecessarily precise timestamps where they would make re-identification easier

Official company statements and broad fandom categories may be discussed because they are necessary to understand the event structure.

## Methodological limits

- The dataset is reconstructed from accessible public traces and is not a complete census of Weibo.
- Deleted or inaccessible posts create survivorship bias.
- Search and archive availability may over-represent posts that were widely linked or indexed.
- A recovered post demonstrates that a statement existed; it does not establish prevalence within a fandom.
- Temporal ordering does not by itself establish causal influence.
- Organizational membership should not be inferred from shared language, hashtags, or similar positions without direct evidence.

## Next steps

The public layer is being redesigned around anonymized and aggregated outputs. Planned work includes:

- structured event timelines
- reproducible data-cleaning scripts
- R / Python analysis notebooks where appropriate
- network and discourse visualization at privacy-safe levels of aggregation
- multilingual source organization
- explicit confidence / evidence-strength fields

The corresponding account-level audit material remains in the private research layer.
