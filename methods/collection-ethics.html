Data Collection, Platform Rules & Research Ethics

This page documents how the June 22-30 reconstruction was assembled, what platform-rule and privacy issues were reviewed, and how the public dataset was transformed to reduce harm and re-identification risk.

Scope of this review

This assessment applies to this specific June 2026 reconstruction. It is not a blanket statement that every possible method of collecting Weibo data is compliant, lawful or ethically equivalent. Any future direct automated collector would require a separate review of its access method, request frequency, current platform rules, privacy risk and applicable law.

This project is an independent fan research project, not an institutional human-subject study and not legal advice. The purpose of this page is transparency: readers should be able to understand how the evidence was found, checked, transformed and published.

Collection route used for this dataset

1. Public-web discoveryCandidate material was located through search-based public-web retrieval, including ChatGPT Search / Deep Research.
2. Source-level checksCandidate records were checked against publicly accessible Sina preservation pages, official Weibo pages and related public traces.
3. Evidence codingDirect text, interpretation, demands, rumors and causal claims were separated instead of being treated as equivalent evidence.
4. Confidence reviewImage-dependent, deleted-original, related-panel-only and otherwise incomplete records were marked as weaker evidence.
5. Public transformationFan identifiers and re-identifying details were removed or generalized before publication.

For the final audit, the working reconstruction contained 217 records. 202 URLs were re-checked directly through preserved Sina pages. The remaining 15 records were separately audited through official Weibo material, preserved related-post views, profile-history evidence or other public traces.

No researcher-operated direct high-frequency Weibo crawler was used to acquire this dataset. The reconstruction relied on search-based public-web discovery and source-level verification rather than scripted login, access-control bypass or a custom bulk scraper making high-frequency requests directly to Weibo.

Platform-rule review: Weibo

Weibo's current Community Convention, Article 54, describes unauthorized automated behavior or activity at a frequency clearly far above normal user behavior - including automated data extraction - as prohibited cheating behavior.

The collection design for this dataset did not use a researcher-operated automated high-frequency extraction process directly against Weibo. That distinction matters because a custom crawler that repeatedly requests Weibo pages would raise a different platform-rule question from search-based retrieval followed by individual public-page verification.

What this does not mean: the project does not claim a formal Weibo approval, a legal determination of compliance, or a general right to scrape public Weibo data. Search-based retrieval also does not automatically make every downstream use permissible.

Privacy and public personal information

Public availability is not treated as equivalent to unlimited re-publication. China's Personal Information Protection Law, Article 27, permits processing of lawfully public personal information only within a reasonable scope, with additional restrictions where a person has explicitly objected or where processing would have a major impact on individual rights and interests.

For that reason, the public layer uses a data-minimization approach:

  • fan usernames and handles are removed;
  • user IDs and post IDs are removed;
  • direct links to individual fan posts are removed;
  • fan quotations are paraphrased rather than reproduced verbatim;
  • minute-level timestamps are generalized where they materially increase re-identification risk;
  • controversial claims are not publicly attributed to individual non-public persons;
  • account-level audit material remains in the private research layer.

Internet-research ethics

The publication design is informed by the Association of Internet Researchers' Internet Research: Ethical Guidelines 3.0. The relevant principle here is not simply whether a post was technically public, but what harms may be created by collecting, aggregating, decontextualizing or making people easier to identify.

In practice, this project applies the following safeguards:

  • Context matters: technically public fandom posts may still carry social expectations that differ from formal public statements by a company or institution.
  • Minimum necessary detail: the public output keeps chronology and analytical meaning while dropping account-level details not needed to support the finding.
  • Re-identification risk: identifiers, direct links, exact fan quotations and some precise timestamps are removed or generalized.
  • Claim separation: "a post made this claim" is kept separate from "the claim is true" and from "the poster intentionally caused the later outcome".
  • Population humility: the reconstructed records are not treated as a representative sample of all Weibo users or all members of a character fandom.

Verification rules used in analysis

The source reconstruction treats the following as separate propositions:

  • an in-game display or phrase existed;
  • a fan post interpreted that display in a particular way;
  • other users relayed or amplified that interpretation;
  • the interpretation was factually correct;
  • the original design was intentional;
  • a specific community or person organized the later spread.

Temporal order can support a reconstruction of sequence, but it does not by itself prove causal influence. Shared language, hashtags or positions do not by themselves prove organizational membership or coordination.

Future automated collection

A future Python or other direct collector would be reviewed separately before use. At minimum, the review would document:

  1. the exact endpoint or page being accessed;
  2. whether login, authentication or access controls are involved;
  3. request frequency and rate limiting;
  4. whether Weibo authorization or an official API is required;
  5. what personal data are retained and for how long;
  6. whether raw identifiers are necessary;
  7. the planned public-output transformation;
  8. current platform rules and applicable privacy-law considerations.
The design principle is simple: collect no more than is needed, make as few direct platform requests as possible, preserve provenance privately, and publish only the level of detail needed to support the public finding.
